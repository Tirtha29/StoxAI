# Capital Gains - Holding Period and Tax-Loss Harvesting

## Long-term vs short-term

Equity holdings are generally taxed at a lower rate if held beyond a
threshold holding period (commonly one year, though exact thresholds vary
by jurisdiction and asset class). Positions sold before crossing the
threshold are taxed as short-term gains, typically at a higher rate.

Investors sitting on an unrealized gain who are within a few weeks of
crossing the long-term threshold often benefit from delaying the sale
until after the threshold date, since the tax rate difference can be
significant relative to the cost of waiting.

## Tax-loss harvesting

Tax-loss harvesting is the practice of selling an investment that has
declined in value to realize a capital loss. The realized loss can offset
realized capital gains elsewhere in the portfolio, and in many
jurisdictions a limited amount of net loss can also offset ordinary
income, with any excess carried forward to future years.

A common pitfall is the "wash sale" style rule in some jurisdictions,
which disallows the loss deduction if a substantially identical security
is repurchased within a short window (commonly 30 days) before or after
the sale. Investors who want to stay invested in the same sector typically
rotate into a similar but not identical security to preserve market
exposure while remaining compliant.

Harvesting is generally more valuable for investors who already have
realized gains elsewhere in the current tax year, since the loss directly
offsets those gains rather than only being available against future gains.
