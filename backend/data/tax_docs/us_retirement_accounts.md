# US Retirement Accounts - 401(k) and IRA

## 401(k)

Contributions to a traditional 401(k) are made pre-tax, reducing taxable
income for the year in which they are made. Employer matches are common
and effectively add free money on top of the employee's own contribution,
subject to an annual employee contribution limit set by the IRS each year.

## IRA

An Individual Retirement Account (IRA) has a separate, generally lower,
annual contribution limit than a 401(k). Traditional IRA contributions may
be tax-deductible depending on income and whether the individual is also
covered by a workplace retirement plan. Roth IRA contributions are made
after-tax but grow and withdraw tax-free in retirement.

## Prioritization for tax savings

A common approach for investors trying to minimize current-year taxable
income: contribute enough to the 401(k) to capture the full employer
match first, since that is an immediate guaranteed return, then consider
filling remaining IRA room, then return to maxing out the 401(k) if
additional room and cash flow allow.

Unused contribution room does not roll over to future years - it resets
each calendar year.
